medicine_name: Paracetamol
aliases: Acetaminophen, Tylenol, Panadol, Calpol, Dolo, Crocin, Mapap, Feverall
category: Pain relief / Fever
used_for: Fever, mild to moderate pain, headache, toothache, muscle aches
overdose_symptoms: Nausea, vomiting, stomach pain, liver damage, jaundice, confusion, coma (delayed 24-72h)
overdose_action: Go to hospital immediately. Do NOT induce vomiting. Antidote must be given within 8 hours for best effect.
max_dose_adult: 1g per dose, max 4g per 24 hours, every 4-6 hours
max_dose_child: 15mg/kg per dose, max 60mg/kg per day
antidote: N-acetylcysteine (NAC) — hospital administration only
warnings: Do not take with alcohol. Do not exceed dose. Dangerous for people with liver disease. Hidden in many combination cold/flu products — check all labels.
safe_in_pregnancy: Yes (low doses, short term)

category: Pain relief / Fever / Anti-inflammatory / Antiplatelet

[022]
medicine_name: Naloxone
aliases: Narcan, Evzio, Nyxoid, Prenoxad
category: Opioid antidote
used_for: Emergency reversal of opioid overdose (heroin, fentanyl, oxycodone, morphine, methadone, codeine, tramadol)
overdose_symptoms: Naloxone itself has almost no toxicity even at high doses; may trigger sudden opioid withdrawal (agitation, vomiting, sweating)
overdose_action: In withdrawal after naloxone: keep the person calm and hydrated; the effect passes within hours. If they became unconscious again, give another naloxone dose.
max_dose_adult: 4mg nasal spray or 0.4-2mg injection, repeat every 2-3 minutes if no response, as many times as needed
max_dose_child: Same weight-based approach: give available nasal spray (4mg) and repeat as needed
antidote: Not applicable (it IS an antidote)
warnings: Naloxone wears off in 30-90 minutes while most opioids last longer — the person can relapse into overdose AFTER seemingly recovering. Always call emergency services even if they wake up. Watch for 2+ hours.
safe_in_pregnancy: Yes (in an overdose emergency, giving naloxone saves both mother and baby)
